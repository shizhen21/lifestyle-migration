READING THE PATTERNS

Differences, with context.

These charts describe how time is spent. They do not establish that age, a demographic trait or a city caused a lifestyle.

Download pattern methods
All respondents first; OD eligibility comes later.

National estimates retain all eligible diaries, including undisclosed geographies. The national analysis retains every observed public metro code. Interactive MSA choices require a matching polygon. Restrictions needed to match ACS migration origins and destinations belong to the later OD analysis.

How are weights and multiple years handled?

Annual estimates use the ATUS final survey weight, which corrects unequal weekday/weekend sampling. Pooled periods give each national year equal weight while retaining each subgroup’s population share. These observed means are not additionally adjusted for month, holidays or demographic composition; the prediction page makes those adjustments.

What do sparse samples and historical codes mean?

Counts are unweighted diaries. Fewer than 30 is a display flag, not an exclusion rule. Missing cells are gaps. Some metro codes are historical; their boundaries changed and are not fully harmonized. Unmatched historical codes remain in the national estimates and are not presented as boundary-matched MSA choices. The 337 codes are not 337 boundary-consistent present-day cities. No confidence intervals are shown.

What belongs to each activity category?

Personal care includes sleep. Work & education includes paid work and study. Household includes care, housework, shopping and services. Social includes socializing, religion and volunteering. Leisure includes eating, exercise and leisure. Travel includes all travel. Unclassified time is retained so that a complete day totals 1,440 minutes.
