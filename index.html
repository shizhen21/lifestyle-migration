THE QUESTION

Different people. Different places. Different days.

Where we live connects to how we spend time. This project measures daily lifestyles in the United States and asks how activity time differs across demographic groups, years and metropolitan areas—and how the places people move to differ from the places they leave.

The American Time Use Survey provides the daily activity diaries. Six broad groups—personal care, work and education, household activities, social activity, leisure and travel—describe a day, with unclassified minutes retained. The interactive activity model uses 215,479 diaries from 2005–2025, excluding the interrupted 2020 survey.

The website brings together observed patterns, standardized model comparisons, individual life-stage scenarios and the existing migration analysis. A profile comparison estimates how a modeled day differs under selected inputs; the ACS evidence describes actual cross-MSA movers. These address related questions, with distinct samples and units.

One life, changing chapters.

The Lifestyle Profiles tool describes model-estimated group averages under selected inputs across life stages and places. Observed population references add context. Place searches either use explicit activity goals or explore patterns in your rated places; neither predicts a causal moving effect.

Actual migration evidence.

The existing OD regressions link ACS 2015–2019 and 2021–2024 movers to prior-year, weekday-adjusted ten-year ATUS area scores. They use 189,114 complete-case adult movers, person weights, year and origin-MSA fixed effects. Their coefficients are associations among movers, rather than personal post-move time changes.
