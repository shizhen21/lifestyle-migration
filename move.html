STEP 3 / YOUR LIFESTYLE PREFERENCES

Choose the day you want. Rank what matters.

Choose Low, Medium or High for each of the six activities, then give each a unique priority from 1 (most important) to 6 (least important). Two activities can both be High; the priority order tells the search which one matters more.

Low, Medium and High mean the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles of the same profile’s modeled minutes across the MSAs that meet your filters. Changing the filters also changes these targets. Targets are aspirations considered together, rather than an independently feasible 24-hour schedule; every candidate day still sums to 1,440 minutes.

THE PATTERN IN YOUR PLACES

Adjust and confirm what matters to you

Positive = more of an activity; negative = less. Larger absolute values give it more importance; zero leaves it out. These are your chosen priorities after adjustment, rather than recovered preferences.

How the examples were measured

For each example, hold its year, age and demographics equal across metros. Express each activity relative to the median and interquartile range of metros observed in that year (minimum scale: one minute). Then compare those place-relative profiles across your examples. These are model contrasts, not your recorded daily activities.

A comparison of model profiles.

Each intensity becomes a percentile target among eligible MSAs. Activities receive weights 6 to 1 according to your priority rank. The score is a weighted distance after scaling each activity by its candidate interquartile range (at least 1 minute), so broad categories do not dominate simply because they take more time. Lower is closer; the score is not a probability. All profile inputs and the diary calendar are held equal across places.
