ABOUT THE ESTIMATES

Which kind of “average day”?

A model-estimated day for your selected year, age, demographics and metro code. Changing several features compares the two complete scenarios.

These differences do not track the same person through time or identify the causal effect of moving.

See observed U.S. patterns first →
What does the full model include?

Demographics, an age spline, annual and metro-code effects, diary weekday, month and holidays; demographic-by-period, age-by-sex, children-by-sex and weekday-by-employment interactions. Seven activity shares are nonnegative and sum to 1,440 minutes. Download the model definition.

What if I leave a feature unspecified?

Unspecified features are averaged over a common, weighted 2021–2025 national reference population, preserving their joint combinations. These are standardized model profiles, rather than raw means for everyone matching your choices. All years use the same 2025 joint weekday, month and holiday calendar.

Which comparisons are possible?

Change year, age, metro or any demographic feature independently. Linking age to year maintains the same birth year; other features remain your choices. Unlink them to compare people of the same age in different years. The interrupted 2020 survey and future years are excluded.

Geographic coverage and limitations

Metro effects are pooled over time, with no city-specific trend. Sparse places can have unstable estimates, and historical boundaries are not fully harmonized. Selecting a year outside a code’s observed span extrapolates its pooled effect. Some demographics come from the earlier CPS interview; income is nominal annual family income. Estimates have no survey-design-consistent confidence intervals.
