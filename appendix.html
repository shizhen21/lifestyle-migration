MEASUREMENT / ATUS ACTIVITY CODEBOOK

What goes into each lifestyle?

The ATUS records a person’s main activity in each diary episode. We add those minutes into six study-defined groups. Sleep is Personal care; eating and drinking are Leisure; cooking is Household & care. The labels describe broad activity groups, not a single behavior.

Personal care

Sleep, washing, dressing and self care.

Sleeping 010101; washing and dressing 010201.

ATUS codes: 01

Work & education

Paid work, work-related tasks, job search, classes and homework.

Main job 050101; taking a class 060101; homework 060301.

ATUS codes: 05, 06

Household & care

Housework, cooking, childcare, helping adults, shopping and obtaining services.

Cooking 020201; childcare 030101; grocery shopping 070101.

ATUS codes: 02, 03, 04, 07, 08, 09, 10

Social

Socializing, social events, religious activities, volunteering and telephone calls.

Talking with others 120101; a party 120201; calling family 160101.

ATUS codes: 14, 15, 16; 1201, 1202; 120501, 120502

Leisure

Eating and drinking, relaxing, TV, reading, hobbies, arts, sports and exercise.

Eating 110101; watching TV 120303; exercise 130134.

ATUS codes: 11, 13; 1203, 1204; 120503, 120504

Travel

Travel for work, shopping, care, leisure and other purposes.

Work travel 180501; grocery-shopping travel 180701.

ATUS codes: 18

Sleeping ≠ all personal care.
Sleep is included, alongside grooming and self care. A Personal care prediction is not a sleep-only prediction.

Eating ≠ preparing food.
Ordinary meals → Leisure; cooking and washing dishes → Household & care.

Commuting ≠ working.
A trip to work → Travel; time actually working → Work & education.

The activity code determines the group.
Eating as part of a job (050202) → Work & education; volunteer food preparation (150201) → Social.

“Household” extends beyond housework.
It also includes care for people outside the household, shopping, medical and other services, and civic obligations.

Unclassified minutes are retained.
Data-quality codes (50), 120599 and 129999 are outside these six groups. This seventh component keeps each day at 1,440 minutes.

Original codebook from the project archive. The BLS ATUS Activity Lexicon, 2003–2025 defines the activities; the six groups above are our research aggregation. The explorer includes all 431 listed six-digit codes. All 425 codes observed in the 2005–2025 analysis (excluding 2020) match the existing classification program. “n.e.c.” means not elsewhere classified. The lexicon’s year notes remain authoritative for historical code changes.

MODEL 1 / PREDICTING ACTIVITY MINUTES

A conditional average day, in minutes.

Inputs

Age, sex, education, race, Hispanic origin, marital status, family income, nativity/citizenship, employment, children, year and public metro code. Weekday, month and holidays control the diary calendar.

Model

A weighted fractional multinomial logit jointly fits seven activity shares, with age splines, period interactions and regularization. The current website uses its full-data v3 fit.

Outputs

Nonnegative expected minutes for every activity, totaling 1,440. A contrast compares two complete profiles under explicitly selected inputs.

If one activity gains a minute…

The remaining activities together lose one minute. The fitted profile determines how that minute is redistributed; no single category is forced to absorb it.

Equation, regularization and reference population
qik = minutesik / 1,440
μik = 1,440 × exp(ηik) / Σj exp(ηij)

Six free logits and an unclassified reference logit of zero. The objective is survey-weight-normalized cross entropy plus λ/2 times squared non-intercept coefficients, with λ = 0.0001. λ is a penalty setting, not an effect size or a reliability statistic.

Age uses fixed cubic-spline knots at 15, 25, 35, 45, 55, 65, 75 and 85. Period terms use 2005–09, 2010–14, 2015–19 and 2021–25. The model also includes demographic×period, age-band×sex, children×sex and weekday×employment interactions. Metro effects are pooled across time.

Unspecified characteristics average over the same 2021–2025 weighted joint reference population, represented by 2,048 deterministic integration points. The calendar averages the real 2025 weekday/month/seven-holiday combinations. Weekday/weekend options condition that calendar.

MODEL 2 / ACTUAL MIGRATION

What differs between destinations and origins?

The original study estimates six metropolitan activity scores in rolling ten-year ATUS windows, adjusts for demographics, year and diary weekday, then links both ends of an ACS move to the window ending in the previous year. Its original area regressions are unweighted; the later ACS regression uses person weights.

ΔSik,t = Sdestination,k,t−1 − Sorigin,k,t−1
= Xi,tβk + μorigin,k + τt,k + εi,k,t

The outcome is a destination-minus-origin area z-score. Coefficients compare demographic groups, controlling origin-MSA and ACS-year fixed effects; main 95% CIs use origin-clustered SEs. They are not a person’s change in daily minutes, a moving probability or a causal moving effect.

Matching rules, sample flow and demographic references

Separate IPUMS PUMA and MIGPUMA crosswalks match current and previous residence. At both endpoints the strict rule requires at least 95% of the PUMA/MIGPUMA population in its assigned MSA and the published combined omission/commission criterion below five percentage points. 2010-based geography is used through 2021 and 2020-based geography from 2022.

402,385 strict cross-MSA movers → 220,817 with both activity profiles → 189,114 complete-case adults. Six PWGTP-weighted OLS models; 79 origin clusters. References: age 18–24, male, married, less than high school, personal income below $25,000, non-Hispanic White, U.S. citizen at birth, employed.

Example: ACS 2015 links to ATUS 2005–2014 at both endpoints. The original selected panel has 121 MSAs and ten windows; 120 match its map. These scores are a different estimator from the newer activity-minute model. ACS personal income is different from ATUS family income.

EXPLORATORY TOOL / PAGE 06

From liked places to a lifestyle pattern.

Each supplied MSA-year-age profile is predicted with the existing activity model. For each activity, subtract the median across boundary-matched metros with diaries in that year and divide by their interquartile range, with a one-minute floor. All metros within that example share its age, year, demographic inputs and day calendar. This removes common time/person levels from the place signal; it does not eliminate all interaction or sparse-area uncertainty.

Examples rated 4 or 5 define a liked-place centroid in six standardized activity dimensions, weighted by rating minus three. Similarity recommendations minimize equal-weight Euclidean distance to that centroid. An unscored residence is not a preference; unchosen cities are not coded as disliked. New candidates all use the one reference profile/year, with the existing region, population and diary-coverage filters. Example places can be excluded.

Tentative preference weights, regularization and sensitivity
minα,β (1/n) Σi [(ratingi − 3)/2 − α − zi′β]² + λ Σk βk²
λ = 1

A fit requires at least four scored examples from three metros, a liked example, a neutral/disliked example and a rating range of at least two. Centering leaves an unpenalized intercept; the six coefficients receive the same fixed ridge penalty. The rating scale is treated as interval-valued for this exploratory approximation. The displayed relative shares are |βk| / Σ|β|, and candidate priority scores are Σβkzjk / Σ|β|. A larger score ranks first. Similarity remains available independently of these coefficients.

Leaving each example out and using penalties 0.25, 1 and 4 checks sign sensitivity. These percentages are not confidence levels or external validation. Identical MSA-year-profile duplicates are rejected. Different years from one metro may be dependent; no independence-based inference is claimed. A few ratings and correlated, compositional activity features do not identify true personal utility weights; job, housing, family and geographic preferences remain omitted. Users can adjust signed priorities explicitly; those are then labeled user-selected. No global activity model or ACS OD regression is refitted.

The browser computes locally. Share links encode the supplied places, demographics and ratings in the URL; profile and result downloads retain the settings. Avoid sharing a link containing details you do not want its recipients to read.

LIMITATIONS

What these comparisons leave unresolved.

One day, substantial variation

Expected minutes describe a conditional average. A person’s actual day can differ greatly; rare demographic combinations and small areas have limited support.

Association and life stage

Repeated cross-sections do not follow individuals or separate causal age, period and cohort effects. Tracks are scenarios; place comparisons do not identify moving effects.

Geography and income

Historical public codes are not fully boundary-harmonized; metro effects are time invariant. Family income is nominal and differs from ACS personal income. Some traits precede the diary.

Inference and validation scope

Survey-design intervals are not implemented for the activity profiles. Random within-year validation tests interpolation, rather than forecasting future years or unseen cities.

Original score reliability

Median split-half correlations of the original metro scores are only 0.11–0.40. Good activity prediction does not repair or independently validate those scores.

Migration specification

City size/density reduces SES travel contrasts. Two-way clusters widen some intervals and needed four covariance-matrix repairs. Strict matching favors larger MSAs.

WITHIN-YEAR RESIDENCE

One year, several places.

Three residences in 2021 default to four months each. Enter six months in New York and six in North Carolina for a 50/50 annual day. Blank durations split the remainder; explicit durations must fill 12 months. The last residence continues into later years without a new chapter.

Annual minutes are the residence-weighted mean of each stage’s predicted day, including its demographics. Each day still totals 1,440 minutes. These are annual estimates averaged by duration, rather than predictions for specific months. Residence shares do not measure how much a person likes a place.
